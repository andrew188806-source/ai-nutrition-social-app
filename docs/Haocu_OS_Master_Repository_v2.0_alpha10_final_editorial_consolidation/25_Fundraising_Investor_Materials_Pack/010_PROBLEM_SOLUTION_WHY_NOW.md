## Problem

Eating is daily, but food decisions are still surprisingly messy.

Users face:

- too many restaurant choices
- generic public ratings
- inaccurate or incomplete nutrition information
- repetitive habits
- shame-based calorie tracking
- poor connection between taste and health context
- social apps that feel unrelated to real-life meal intent

Restaurants face:

- noisy discovery channels
- limited ability to show dish-level nutrition/context
- difficulty reaching users whose preferences actually fit their food
- dependency on ads, delivery platforms, and public ratings

## Solution

Haocu creates a meal-centered loop:

1. Analyze or record what the user ate.
2. Let the user correct the AI output.
3. Store the corrected record as private food memory.
4. Recommend the next meal with taste and nutrition context.
5. Connect to restaurant data.
6. Optionally create a Meal Buddy or group table.

## Why Now

Several shifts make this possible:

- AI can reduce the friction of meal logging.
- Consumers are familiar with food apps but still lack personalization.
- Restaurants need better digital menu/data infrastructure.
- Users increasingly want private utility rather than public posting.
- Social discovery works better when connected to real-world context.

## Why Haocu’s Timing Is Specific

Haocu can begin with a narrow MVP and use correction as a trust-building mechanism. Instead of waiting for perfect AI, the product turns imperfect AI into a collaborative workflow.

## Key Message

Haocu is useful even before it is perfect. Every correction makes the product more personal.
