## Script

Hi, I’m building **Haocu**, an AI food recommendation app for people in Taiwan who eat out often.

The daily problem is simple: every day people ask, “What should I eat?” But the tools they use are fragmented. Google reviews show what many people liked, not what *I* will like. Calorie apps are often manual and shame-based. Social apps start from profiles instead of real intent. And restaurant information is often not structured enough for personal recommendation.

Haocu starts with the meal itself.

A user takes a photo or records what they ate. The AI suggests possible dishes and nutrition estimates, but we do not pretend the AI is perfect. The user can correct the result. That corrected meal becomes part of their private food diary and taste memory.

Once Haocu understands recent meals, preferences, and context, it can recommend the next meal more personally: maybe something lighter after a heavy lunch, something higher protein, or a restaurant that fits the user’s taste instead of just the public average.

The second layer is optional social dining. If the user wants company, they can turn a meal or restaurant into a Meal Buddy card: what they want to eat, when, where, and whether they prefer to chat first or eat directly. Free users can stay anonymous with mascot cards. Premium users can unlock more limits and real-person cards after verification.

For restaurants, Haocu creates a structured menu and nutrition data layer. Restaurants can provide clearer dish information, QR nutrition pages, and become discoverable through better matching rather than pure advertising.

Our MVP focuses on four loops:

1. AI meal analysis and correction.
2. Food diary and nutrition summary.
3. Personalized next-meal and restaurant recommendation.
4. Optional Meal Buddy and group dining.

The business model starts with consumer Premium, restaurant partner packages, and crowdfunding/early supporter bundles. Longer-term phases include restaurant supply-chain and household nutrition, but those are not MVP promises.

The reason this can become defensible is the corrected data loop: meal photos, user corrections, taste memory, restaurant menu graph, and food-intent social behavior. Generic food content is easy to copy; a private user-specific food memory and local restaurant data graph are harder to replicate.

The current milestone is to stabilize the MVP demo, run closed pilots, onboard early restaurants, validate willingness to pay, and prepare a crowdfunding campaign. We are looking for investors, advisors, and partners who can help with engineering execution, product validation, restaurant access, legal/IP review, and early fundraising.

## Closing Line

Haocu is not trying to be another calorie app or another dating app. It is trying to become the personal food operating system for daily outside eaters: what you ate, what you like, what you should eat next, and who you may want to eat with.
