# Investor Objection Response Bank

> Use: Prepare repeatable answers without sounding defensive.

## Objection 1: "This feels too broad."

**Response:** That is a fair concern. The long-term platform has several layers, but the MVP is intentionally narrow: AI meal analysis, private taste memory, next-meal recommendation, and limited meal-buddy intent. The next funding milestone is to prove that daily loop, not to build every expansion layer.

## Objection 2: "Food recognition is hard."

**Response:** Yes. That is why Haocu is designed with editable results, candidate options, and database-first logic. We do not need perfect AI on day one; we need a correction loop that users tolerate and that improves personalization.

## Objection 3: "Users do not want to track food."

**Response:** Heavy tracking is a problem. Haocu is designed to make tracking lighter by starting from photo/upload and turning the output into future recommendations, not just a static calorie log.

## Objection 4: "Why would users pay?"

**Response:** Payment must be validated. The likely paid value is extended memory, deeper recommendations, premium identity/social features, and goal tools. The next step is willingness-to-pay and crowdfunding/prelaunch evidence.

## Objection 5: "Restaurants already have Google Maps and delivery platforms."

**Response:** Those platforms are important, but they are not built around private taste memory and nutrition-aware meal choice. Haocu can start as a consumer utility and later offer restaurants structured insight and targeted discovery.

## Objection 6: "Social matching introduces risk."

**Response:** Agreed. Social is optional and should be phased carefully with verification, reporting, limited cards, and conservative UX. The product can still deliver value through food memory and recommendation even before social scale.

## Objection 7: "Where is the traction?"

**Response:** Current stage is demo/MVP preparation. We are not claiming live traction unless evidence exists. Alpha 9 is designed to track demo, pilot, live, and verified evidence separately so the next financing is based on clear milestones.

## Objection 8: "This needs professional review."

**Response:** Correct. The repository includes a professional review pack covering legal/IP, privacy, terms, health/nutrition claims, finance/tax, fundraising/securities, restaurant commercial issues, and cross-border considerations. Sensitive claims will be reviewed before broad external use.

## Objection 9: "What is the moat?"

**Response:** The potential moat is not a generic AI model. It is repeated user taste/correction data, meal history, local restaurant/menu data, recommendation feedback, and social food context. The pilot must prove that this data improves user experience.

## Objection 10: "Why you?"

**Response:** The founder advantage is the ability to synthesize food behavior, AI personalization, social meal context, and restaurant operations into one coherent product loop, then iterate quickly. The next proof point is execution: MVP build, pilot evidence, and focused scope control.
