# Master Diligence Q&A

> Use: Main answer bank for investor, advisor, accelerator, and platform diligence.  
> Rule: Do not improvise sensitive answers in meetings. Use this file, then update it after each investor conversation.

## 1. Company and Stage

### Q1. What exactly is Haocu?
Haocu is an AI-powered food decision platform for outside-eating users. The product combines meal photo analysis, private taste memory, nutrition-aware recommendations, restaurant discovery, and optional meal-buddy matching.

### Q2. Is Haocu a nutrition app, restaurant app, or social app?
The wedge is food decision intelligence. The MVP uses AI nutrition and restaurant recommendation as the core utility, with social matching as an optional engagement loop. It should not be positioned as a pure diet app or pure dating app.

### Q3. What stage is the company at?
Current stage should be described as demo / MVP preparation unless live product metrics exist. The repository is mature as a planning and investor-readiness system, but traction claims should only be made when supported by actual demo, pilot, or live evidence.

### Q4. What is the immediate fundraising goal?
The immediate goal is to fund MVP execution, professional review, pilot operations, and early market validation. The exact amount, instrument, valuation, and jurisdiction must be confirmed before external use.

## 2. Product and User

### Q5. Who is the first user?
The initial user is a Taiwan outside-eating consumer who frequently chooses meals from restaurants, convenience stores, food courts, delivery, or casual dining and wants better recommendations without heavy manual tracking.

### Q6. What is the first daily use case?
The user takes or uploads a meal photo, checks the AI analysis, corrects it if needed, saves the meal, and later receives a more personalized next-meal suggestion.

### Q7. Why would users keep coming back?
Return behavior should come from private taste memory, food diary continuity, next-meal recommendations, saved restaurants, and optional social meal intent. The product must prove this through retention metrics.

### Q8. Why include social at all?
Food is a natural social context, and meal intent lowers the pressure of generic matching. However, the MVP should keep social optional and bounded so the product does not become over-scoped.

### Q9. What should be cut if scope is too large?
Protect the core loop first: AI meal analysis, correction, taste memory, next-meal recommendation, restaurant selection. Meal-buddy social can remain as a limited demo/MVP feature, while group dining and advanced restaurant analytics can be phased.

## 3. AI and Data

### Q10. What does the AI actually do?
The AI helps identify meals, infer ingredients, estimate nutrition, suggest corrections, and support recommendations. It should remain assistive, transparent, and editable by the user.

### Q11. How do you avoid inaccurate nutrition claims?
Use conservative language, present estimates, allow user correction, avoid medical treatment claims, and route health/nutrition claims through professional review.

### Q12. What is the database-first AI policy?
When structured restaurant/menu/ingredient data exists, Haocu should rely on the database first. AI should fill gaps, propose candidates, and learn from corrections rather than hallucinating unsupported certainty.

### Q13. Is there a defensible data moat?
Potentially, if users repeatedly correct, rate, save, and choose meals. The moat is not a generic food recognition model; it is personalized taste and context data connected to local restaurant/menu structures.

### Q14. What data privacy issues matter most?
Food photos, health-adjacent nutrition data, social profiles, chat/invite data, location context, and restaurant interactions require careful consent, retention, security, and deletion policies.

## 4. Market and Competition

### Q15. What market are you entering?
Haocu enters the intersection of food discovery, nutrition tracking, AI personalization, and social dining. For investor discussion, use concrete beachhead segmentation rather than claiming every adjacent market immediately.

### Q16. Who are competitors?
Competitors include restaurant review/discovery platforms, nutrition trackers, AI meal recognition apps, dating/social apps, delivery apps, and restaurant marketing tools. Haocu differentiates by integrating private taste memory with meal recommendation and optional food-based social intent.

### Q17. Why won't Google Maps or delivery apps just do this?
They may improve recommendations, but their incentives and data structures are different. Haocu's wedge is personal food memory across meals, nutrition context, and social meal intent rather than only restaurant search or delivery conversion.

### Q18. Why now?
AI usage has become mainstream enough for food-photo workflows, while consumers still face repetitive meal choice and restaurants still need better structured digital demand channels.

## 5. Business Model and Unit Economics

### Q19. How will Haocu make money?
Near-term paths include consumer premium, restaurant profiles/pilots, and crowdfunding/early supporter packages. Longer-term paths include restaurant analytics, campaigns, family nutrition, and supply-chain/ESG workflows.

### Q20. What will users pay for?
Users may pay for more recommendation depth, more meal-buddy matching, longer memory, advanced insights, premium identity, goal mode, and convenience. This must be validated through willingness-to-pay tests.

### Q21. What will restaurants pay for?
Restaurants may pay for verified menu profiles, nutrition pages, campaign support, pilot traffic, analytics, and structured customer insight. This must be validated through restaurant meetings and LOIs.

### Q22. What are the cost risks?
AI inference, image storage, engineering labor, customer support, trust/safety, marketing acquisition, legal/privacy review, and restaurant onboarding can all create cost pressure.

## 6. Go-To-Market

### Q23. How do you get the first users?
Start with founder-led demos, food personality test / mascot content, crowdfunding prelaunch, university/young professional communities, outside-eating users, and restaurant pilot partners.

### Q24. How do restaurants enter the loop?
Initially, through lightweight pilot cards, menu/nutrition data setup, demo pages, and co-marketing. Do not require full enterprise onboarding for MVP.

### Q25. What is the first traction milestone?
A realistic first milestone is not massive user scale. It is proof that users complete meal analysis, save/correct meals, return for recommendation, and express meal-buddy or restaurant intent.

## 7. Legal / IP / Compliance

### Q26. Is there patent protection?
Patent and IP strategy should be discussed as review-ready, not concluded. The repository contains legal/IP preparation materials, but any external patent claim needs professional review.

### Q27. Are there health or medical risks?
Yes. Nutrition estimation can be health-adjacent. Haocu should avoid diagnosis, treatment, disease management, or guaranteed health outcomes unless reviewed and explicitly scoped.

### Q28. What about social safety?
Meal matching requires verification, reporting, blocking, privacy controls, conservative invite flows, and phased rollout. The MVP should limit risk by keeping social features bounded.

### Q29. What about securities/fundraising compliance?
Any public fundraising, crowdfunding, SAFE/equity offering, or cross-border investment structure needs legal review. This pack is not an offering document.

## 8. Team and Execution

### Q30. What team is needed next?
Likely needs include MVP engineering, product/design, AI/data support, legal/privacy review, restaurant pilot operations, and growth/crowdfunding execution. Hiring should be milestone-based.

### Q31. What is the founder advantage?
Founder advantage should be framed around deep product iteration, market observation, ability to synthesize AI/nutrition/social/restaurant workflows, and speed of repository/product definition. Avoid unsupported claims.

### Q32. What is the biggest execution risk?
Scope control. Haocu can become too broad. The mitigation is to prove one narrow daily loop before expanding into the full platform vision.

## 10. Closing Questions

### Q36. What would make this investable?
A tight MVP, clear retention loop, credible pilot evidence, disciplined claims, professional review of sensitive areas, and a founder/team capable of executing quickly.

### Q37. What would make you pause?
Unsupported traction claims, uncontrolled social safety risk, unreviewed medical/legal claims, too many simultaneous business models, or inability to prove users return after first meal analysis.
