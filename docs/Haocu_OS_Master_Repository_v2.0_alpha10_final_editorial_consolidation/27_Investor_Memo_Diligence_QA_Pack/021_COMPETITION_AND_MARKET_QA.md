# Competition and Market Q&A

## Q1. What category should investors put Haocu in?
The cleanest category is AI food decision intelligence, with consumer AI, food discovery, nutrition tracking, and social dining as adjacent categories.

## Q2. Why is this not just a restaurant review app?
Restaurant review apps rank places based on public signals. Haocu aims to use private taste memory, recent intake, and food context to recommend what a specific user should eat next.

## Q3. Why is this not just a calorie tracker?
Calorie trackers focus on logging and goals. Haocu uses nutrition as one part of a broader meal decision loop: analyze, remember, recommend, and optionally connect.

## Q4. Why is this not just a dating app?
Meal-buddy is not the only product and should not be positioned as dating-first. It is an optional social layer around meal intent.

## Q5. Why can a startup win against large platforms?
A startup can focus on a narrower, emotionally specific workflow that larger platforms may not prioritize: daily personal food memory connected to Taiwan outside-eating behavior and local restaurant data.

## Q6. What is the wedge market?
The wedge is users who frequently eat outside and are dissatisfied with generic restaurant reviews, repetitive meal choice, and heavy nutrition tracking.

## Q7. What expansion paths exist after MVP?
- deeper consumer premium
- restaurant profiles and analytics
- group dining and social food communities
- crowdfunding/IP/mascot community
- ESG/supply-chain workflows
- family nutrition and home meal planning

## Q8. What competitive evidence is needed?
- user interviews showing pain with current apps
- side-by-side recommendation comparison
- willingness-to-pay survey
- restaurant partner feedback
- retention metrics showing Haocu becomes a habit
