# Target Users

## Primary Target

The primary Haocu user is a Taiwan external diner who frequently eats outside, wants better food decisions, and does not want to manually log every detail.

This includes students, office workers, founders, freelancers, gym-aware but non-professional users, and urban diners who rotate through convenience stores, lunch boxes, noodle shops, cafes, bento restaurants, brunch shops, and casual restaurants.

## Primary User Segments

### 1. Busy External Diner

**Profile**

- Eats out frequently.
- Makes meal decisions quickly.
- Does not want to manually calculate nutrition.
- Often repeats familiar meals because deciding is tiring.

**Pain**

- Does not know whether today’s intake is balanced.
- Public ratings do not explain whether a restaurant fits personal taste.
- Healthy choices feel repetitive or inconvenient.

**Haocu Value**

- Take a photo, get a quick estimate.
- See simple nutrition summary.
- Receive next-meal recommendation based on today’s intake and taste.

### 2. Health-Aware but Non-Expert User

**Profile**

- Wants to eat better but is not a dietitian.
- May care about weight, protein, sugar, fiber, or balanced meals.
- Does not need medical precision.

**Pain**

- Nutrition apps are too technical.
- “Good” and “bad” food language creates pressure.
- Meal tracking becomes boring after a few days.

**Haocu Value**

- Friendly guidance.
- No shame language.
- Balance suggestions instead of punishment.
- Premium health-goal mode for users who want more structure.

### 3. Taste-Driven Explorer

**Profile**

- Likes discovering restaurants.
- Distrusts overly generic ratings.
- Wants recommendations closer to personal taste.

**Pain**

- Google ratings can be inflated, generic, or not aligned with personal taste.
- Popular places may not fit user preference.
- Hard to remember which dishes were actually good.

**Haocu Value**

- Private food ratings become recommendation input.
- Similar-taste user data can improve discovery.
- Food diary stores personal favorites and Top lists.

### 4. Social Diner

**Profile**

- Wants to eat with others sometimes.
- Does not want a dating-app pressure environment.
- May prefer chat-first before meeting.

**Pain**

- Finding someone to eat with is awkward.
- Meal intent is often unclear in general social apps.
- Safety and cancellation boundaries matter.

**Haocu Value**

- Meal-buddy card states restaurant/food/time/payment/chat preference.
- Anonymous mascot card for free users.
- Real-person card and richer social identity for premium/verified users.
- One-on-one and group table flows remain food-first.

### 5. Restaurant Partner

**Profile**

- Restaurant wants visibility and better customer understanding.
- May not have structured nutrition data yet.
- May later want campaign, surplus, or ESG tools.

**Pain**

- Consumer discovery is expensive.
- Menu information is not structured for nutrition or personalization.
- Paid advertising can feel untrusted if not transparent.

**Haocu Value**

- Restaurant card and dish surface.
- User AI uploads can generate demand signals.
- Future nutrition disclosure and restaurant dashboard.
- Future surplus/dynamic pricing operations.

## Jobs To Be Done

| Situation | User Job | Haocu Response |
|---|---|---|
| I just ate something | Help me understand it quickly | AI meal analysis and save flow |
| I need dinner ideas | Tell me what fits today | Next-meal recommendation |
| I want to eat nearby | Find places that fit me | Restaurant recommendation |
| I want company | Help me express meal intent safely | Meal-buddy card |
| I want to remember food | Store my ratings privately | Food diary and favorites |
| I am a restaurant | Help my dishes become discoverable | Restaurant card/menu data |

## Non-Primary Users for MVP

The MVP should not optimize for:

- clinical nutrition patients requiring professional supervision;
- competitive athletes needing precision macro plans;
- hospitals, schools, or enterprise cafeteria deployments;
- full restaurant POS replacement;
- complex family grocery planning;
- professional medical diagnosis or treatment;
- fully automated dating or high-risk social discovery.

These users may become future segments only after product safety, compliance, and operational foundations are mature.

## Early Adopter Hypothesis

Best early adopters are users who already do at least one of the following:

- take photos of food;
- care about protein/calories/fiber but dislike strict tracking;
- use Google Maps or Instagram for restaurant discovery;
- enjoy light social apps;
- are willing to try a cute, AI-assisted food product;
- like mascot or personality-test style shareables.

## User Research Questions

Before beta, validate:

1. Do users understand the difference between meal analysis and food diary?
2. Do users want restaurant recommendation before or after analysis?
3. Which social boundary feels safest: chat-first, direct meal, group table, or no social?
4. How much correction effort is acceptable after AI analysis?
5. Which premium benefit feels most valuable: real-person card, more recommendations, Top saved items, or health-goal mode?
6. Do restaurants understand why structured dish data matters?
